Keeping Your Pleasanton Water Heater Running Longer

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, lift the T&P valve lever once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on descaling once a year in hard-water areas.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters run 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models usually run 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing warning signs such as discolored hot water, rumbling during heating, moisture around the base, or showers that go cold sooner, a planned replacement is nearly always less expensive and far less stressful than an emergency one after the tank lets go.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Consider a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a short, affordable repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water starts eating the tank steel, and just like that a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a flooded utility room.
