No Surprises

Honest, Upfront Pricing

Good water heater service starts with knowing exactly what you're dealing with. Dublin Water Heater offers honest, upfront pricing for every home, covering everything from a burned-out heating element to a full tank replacement. Every visit begins with a careful inspection of your water heater, including its age, capacity, fuel type, venting, and the condition of the tank and valves, followed by a plain-English recommendation with the price spelled out before any work starts.

The problem with water heater failures is that most of them develop inside the tank where you can't see them. Minerals build a crust on the tank floor, the anode rod wears down gradually, and once it's gone, the tank itself starts to corrode. When hot water comes out rusty, the tank rumbles, or you spot moisture under the heater, have it checked before a repair turns into a replacement.

Not every visit is about a breakdown. Plenty of homeowners call us to evaluate an aging heater before it fails, and it's a smart move. With a typical tank lifespan of 8 to 12 years (tankless units often run 20 years with regular descaling), a replacement on your own timeline beats scrambling for a new heater with water on the floor. We'll give you an honest read on how much life your equipment has left.

Maintenance Plans

Tank Flushing and Tune-Ups That Pay Off

Each maintenance visit from Dublin Water Heater is far more than opening the drain valve. Our technician flushes the sediment, pulls and inspects the anode rod, tests the relief valve, checks the burner, pilot, and venting on gas heaters, checks elements and thermostats on electric models, and sets a safe water temperature. Small problems are caught while they're still cheap to fix, and your water heater stays running clean and efficient.

Most homes do best with one flush a year, and homes with hard water may need two. We adjust the schedule to the age of your equipment, how hard your household works it, and local water quality, which determines how fast sediment builds up and how long an anode rod lasts.

Homeowners are often surprised how much a regular maintenance schedule pays off. A clean tank heats water faster and more quietly, a healthy anode rod protects the steel from corrosion, and a working relief valve protects the whole house. Most families find that a maintenance plan costs far less than replacing a water heater even one year early.

Sized for Your Household

A Water Heater Sized for Your Actual Household

Bigger isn't always better when it comes to water heaters. What matters for a tank is the first-hour rating, how many gallons of hot water it can deliver in one busy hour, and for a tankless unit it's the flow rate it can sustain at your incoming water temperature. That's why we never just copy the size of the old unit. We look at how many people live in the home, how many bathrooms you have, and big draws like a soaking tub or a high-capacity washer.

We walk you through the options for every home, especially growing families with rising hot water demands. A 40-gallon tank that served a couple rarely holds out once kids are showering back to back, and a tankless water heater can deliver continuous hot water so long as it's sized for the flow. If you're weighing tank against tankless, or gas against a heat pump model, our technician will lay out the honest trade-offs: upfront cost, operating cost, gas line and venting requirements, electrical service, and space, so you can pick what fits your home and budget.

Repairs We Handle

Common Water Heater Problems We Fix in Dublin

No hot water, a burner that refuses to light, a tank full of sediment, a weeping relief valve: every one of these problems has a different cause and calls for its own repair. Dublin Water Heater diagnoses the exact fault before swapping any parts, whether it's an electric tank with a burned-out element, a gas heater with a failing thermocouple, a broken dip tube mixing cold water into the hot, or a tankless unit throwing error codes.

Some problems are mostly a nuisance, while others pose real risks. A burned-out element simply leaves you with lukewarm water, but a relief valve that keeps discharging is a warning that pressure or temperature in the tank is running too high, and a thermostat set above 120°F can scald a child in seconds. If you ever smell gas near the heater, leave the house and call your gas utility from outside before you call us. We match the fix to the failure: elements and thermostats on electric tanks, thermocouples, flame sensors, and gas valves on gas units, expansion tanks where water pressure is spiking, and leak repair on drain valves, fittings, and relief lines.
